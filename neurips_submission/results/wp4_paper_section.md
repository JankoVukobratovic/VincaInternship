# WP4 results text for the paper (numbers from results/wp4_summary.txt, run of 2026-08-28)

## Method paragraph: closing the loop

The audit statistics of WP2 are summary statistics, so they support likelihood-free inference: rejection ABC over the simulator's own knobs, from the single real tilted scan and nothing else. The prior is the calibration-uncertainty jitter of WP1 extended with the one discrete defect of our history, the v1 bilinear sampling, at prior probability 0.5, so the loop must reject our real mistake by itself. For each of 3000 draws we simulate the tilted measurement, evaluate the nine-statistic battery against the real scan, whiten by the session-aware null of WP2, and accept the closest 5 % by RMS z (2 % and 10 % shown as sensitivity). Twelve accepted draws then train a posterior ensemble with the WP1 seeds and budget, evaluated exactly as the WP1 ensembles.

## Results paragraph: the posterior

The posterior concentrates on a simulator that is noisier than calibrated (noise scale 1.43 +- 0.35 against the prior's 1.07 +- 0.36), slightly rotated (+0.15 +- 0.17 deg) and shifted (+0.08 +- 0.06 px) in registration, with gains essentially at calibration; the bilinear sampling is rejected completely (posterior probability 0.000 at every acceptance level, prior 0.51): the loop re-discovers the v2 correction without being told. The nominal cubic simulator sits at whitened distance 2.7 from the real scan, the nominal bilinear at 8.9. The posterior predictive check is honest about what remains: no posterior draw is indistinguishable from the real scan (0/12 "ok"), but the verdict is uniformly "noise", and the decomposition shows why: the posterior clears the high-frequency flag (aggregate z 1.9 against the threshold 2.05, nominal 3.0) and halves the noise-constant misfit (2.45 vs 3.38) but cannot move the per-line contrast pattern (4.6), because the real noise excess is line-dependent (variance ratios 0.9 to 4.6 across lines) and the knob family only has a global noise scale. The check thus names the next knob the simulator needs, per-line noise scales, which is exactly what closing the loop is for.

## Results paragraph: the posterior ensemble

On the real scan the posterior ensemble is the best restoration in the study: mean r 0.9539 against 0.9527 for the prior ensemble, 0.9526 for the physics inverse, 0.9496 for the control ensemble and 0.9485 for the single nominal net, at or above the physics inverse on 7 of 8 lines (Cu within 0.0003), with the smallest level bias (1.07 %) and restored contrast (0.982). Its uncertainty band is 27 % narrower than the prior's (spread 77 counts against 106) at essentially preserved coverage (0.85 against 0.87 at 2 sigma, 0.94 against 0.95 at 3 sigma, both far above the control's 0.80 and 0.91) and with a smaller actual error (rms 223 against 228). On the simulated held-out cases, where the nominal simulator is the true one by construction, the posterior sits between control and prior everywhere, as it should. One measurement therefore bought a simulator posterior that makes the ensemble narrower, more accurate and equally honest on reality; the part of the discrepancy it could not absorb is precisely localised for the next calibration campaign.

## Results paragraph: one proper score (NLL)

Coverage, ranking and band width are separate dials; the per-pixel Gaussian negative log likelihood is one proper score that rewards a band which is both narrow and calibrated, and it does not flatter us. On the real scan (band = spread + propagated noise + reference noise) the prior ensemble scores best (6.45 nats), the posterior second (6.58) and the control far behind (7.27); the posterior's 27 % narrower band and better mean do not fully pay for its 1 to 2 points of lost coverage, and the lines where it loses most to the prior (Ca, PbLa, PbLb) are exactly the lines with the largest unmodelled per-line noise excess, in full agreement with the predictive check. Inside the dropout hole the ordering inverts sharply (control 11.0, posterior 15.0, prior 15.5): the score punishes the confidently wrong fills of the perturbation-trained ensembles, quantifying the WP1 warning that simulator uncertainty is not prior uncertainty. The honest statement of the loop is therefore: one measurement bought a narrower band, a better mean and the best bias, at a small cost in the proper score that is precisely attributable to the misfit the posterior is known to retain.

## Results paragraph: the second turn of the loop

Since the first posterior predictive check named a missing knob, we added it: round 2 extends the prior with per-line noise multipliers (log-sd 0.6, wide enough to cover the measured per-line variance ratios of 0.9 to 4.6) and reruns the same ABC. The posterior moves the per-line noise where the pair-based fidelity check put it, most strongly on the Pb L lines (effective scale 1.9 to 2.2 on PbLa and PbLb at 5 % acceptance) and on Cu, and keeps rejecting the bilinear sampling. But the fit barely deepens: the best whitened distance improves from 2.16 to 2.02 against a floor of 0.95 +- 0.24 (the null pairs' own distance), and the predictive check still rejects every posterior draw. The honest conclusion of the second turn is that the remaining discrepancy is not expressible by the noise-knob family either; it points at the structures the forward model's documentation itself lists as unmodelled (per-pixel flat-field, scatter background, intra-scan dwell drift), and at the limits of rejection ABC in 22 dimensions with 3000 draws. The loop does not converge to "perfect" in two turns; it converges to a precise statement of what is missing.

## The three sentences

Rejection ABC over the simulator's calibration knobs, using the audit battery as summary statistics and one real scan as data, rejects the v1 bilinear sampling completely (posterior probability 0.000 from a prior of 0.5) and concentrates on a noisier, slightly mis-registered instrument. The ensemble retrained on twelve posterior draws is the most accurate restoration in the study on the real scan (r 0.9539, at or above the physics inverse on 7 of 8 lines) with a 27 % narrower band than the prior ensemble at essentially preserved coverage, though by the proper NLL score the wider prior band remains marginally better (6.45 vs 6.58 nats), the difference sitting on the lines whose noise the knob family cannot express. The posterior predictive check still flags a residual, uniformly diagnosed as noise; adding the knob it names (per-line noise, round 2) moves the marginals in the measured direction but does not close the gap (best whitened distance 2.0 against a null floor of 0.95), so the loop ends with a precise statement of what the simulator still lacks: unmodelled structure, not mis-set knobs.

## Honest caveats

1. ABC with 9 x 8 summary statistics and 5 % acceptance on 3000 draws is coarse; the marginals are indicative, not sharp posteriors, and the sensitivity to the acceptance fraction is reported (2 / 5 / 10 % agree on every direction). Round 2 (22 free parameters) is at the edge of what rejection ABC resolves; an SMC sampler is the obvious upgrade and is future work.
2. The summaries were designed for the audit, not proven sufficient for inference; a systematically wrong statistic would bias the posterior.
3. The posterior ensemble's coverage is 1 to 2 points below the prior's and its NLL is 0.13 nats worse; we report the tradeoff (narrower and more accurate vs marginally better proper score) rather than claiming dominance in either direction.
4. The whole loop uses one real scan; the posterior inherits any peculiarity of that acquisition.
