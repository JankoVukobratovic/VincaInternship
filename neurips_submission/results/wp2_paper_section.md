## Method paragraph: blind simulator diagnostics

We ask whether the discrepancy between a measurement and its simulation can be attributed to a component of the simulator without ground truth. A battery of eight per-line statistics compares a "real" tilted map R with its simulation S: level ratio, contrast (CV) ratio, high-frequency (f > 0.6 Nyquist) and mid-frequency (0.15 to 0.40 Nyquist) power ratios, a single-map noise-constant estimate ratio (residual to a 3 x 3 mean), the signed sub-pixel cross-correlation offsets dy, dx, and a rotation estimate from the differential vertical offset of the two half-frames. The null distribution is the crucial choice: with pairs that differ only by noise, every statistic fires on the real scan (a 0.3 deg registration residual gives z = 60), because the question "is anything different?" is always answered yes. We therefore build the null from 24 pairs whose "real" side is simulated with knobs drawn within the calibration uncertainty (config.JITTER, the same draw that defines the WP1 ensemble) and whose simulation side is nominal; a flag then means "the discrepancy exceeds what the simulator itself claims to know", and defects inside the calibration uncertainty are undetectable by construction. The decision rule (rotation > shift > level > mid-frequency power > high-frequency/noise/contrast, each at null mean + 3 sd of the line-averaged |z|) was fixed before the ladder was run. Gain and angle-belief errors are degenerate at a single angle (both scale g - 1) and are scored as one family.

## Results paragraph: blind identification

On 18 defective simulators x 5 noise seeds x 2 angles (7.7 and 20 deg) plus 48 nominal pairs, the pre-registered rule identifies the resampling blur (our v1 mistake) 10/10, registration shifts down to 0.5 px 30/30, registration rotations of 1 and 2 deg 20/20, and noise-constant errors of x0.25, x2 and x4 30/30, with 4/48 false alarms on nominal pairs; the x0.5 noise rung (2.1 sigma of the calibrated log-uncertainty) is invisible (1/10). The gain family is missed entirely (0/80): a level change that sits on the two or three lines with a large tilt response is diluted by the average over eight lines. A post-hoc template statistic, the projection of the per-line level deviation on the simulator's own tilt-response vector g - 1 (an estimate of the slope error), recovers the extreme rungs (gain x0 and x2: 20/20) but not x0.5, x1.5 or an angle belief off by 5 deg (0/60), all of which lie within the 20 % slope uncertainty the calibration already admits. The leave-one-out nearest-centroid classifier on the z-vectors reaches the same grouped accuracy (73/114 per angle), so the failures are a limit of the statistics, not of the rule. Reading: the components whose signature is spatial (blur, shift, rotation) or spectral (noise) are identifiable from one scan; the components whose signature is a per-line level (gains, angle) are identifiable only when they exceed the calibration uncertainty, and gain-versus-angle needs a second tilt angle to break the degeneracy.

## Results paragraph: the real scan

Applied to the measured tilted scan against its simulation from prova1, with a null that also contains the session difference (the "real" side simulated from prova2), the battery returns exactly the history of the project. Against the v1 bilinear simulator and against the validated bilinear emulator it flags contrast, high- and mid-frequency power and the noise constant (z = 5 to 15; real/sim high-frequency power 1.7 to 2.1 per line) and names the defect "blur": the simulator is blurrier than the instrument, which is the mismatch that made the v1 net over-sharpen. Against the v2 cubic simulator the mid-frequency band and the contrast are quiet (z = 0.6 and 1.0), the registration is inside the calibration sigma (dy = -0.03 px, rotation 0.27 deg), and what remains is a noise excess: the real scan carries 18 % more high-frequency power and a 19 % larger noise constant than the v2 simulation (z = 3.0 and 3.6), the same direction as the sd_real > sd_pred of the fidelity check. The blind procedure therefore re-discovers the blur mismatch we found by hand and tells us what the next iteration of the simulator should fix.

## Results paragraph: defect tolerance

One net per broken simulator, 18 rungs over six defect families, all with the seed of one nominal control member, so that rung-to-rung differences are the simulator and the 12 nominal WP1 control members give the initialisation band (delta r within [+0.082, +0.090] on the simulated footprint, [-0.0052, -0.0021] on the real scan). The training simulator turns out to be remarkably tolerant along every axis that is not geometric. The noise constant can be wrong by a factor of 4 in either direction and the delivered restoration stays inside the seed band on the simulated footprint (delta r +0.085 to +0.090) and within 0.005 of it on the real scan; the tilt-gain slope can be doubled or removed entirely, and the angle belief off by 5 deg, with no material effect (all inside or within 0.0002 of the band). Geometry is different: a registration shift of 0.5 px, five times the calibration uncertainty but half a pixel, already costs delta r 0.035 on the simulated footprint (four seeds: +0.050 to +0.058 against the nominal band's +0.082 to +0.090) and 0.007 on the real scan (four seeds all negative, -0.013 to -0.005); at 1 px the net is worse than the physics inverse it refines (delta r -0.057 sim, -0.086 real), and at 2 px it destroys the restoration (-0.23, -0.27). A rotation of 1 deg is a mild loss (-0.019 real), 2 deg a clear one (-0.047, contrast 0.963). The blur rung reproduces the v1 history in one line: on the simulated testbed it sits exactly on the nominal band (+0.083 to +0.089 over four seeds, because the validated test emulator shares the bilinear acquisition), while on the real scan it is the only non-geometric rung that leaves the band, and it does so for every one of four seeds (delta r -0.012 to -0.006), with the only contrast ratio that overshoots 1 (1.013, the over-sharpening signature that first exposed the mismatch). The hole region is excluded from these statements: its seed band spans [-0.03, +0.27], initialisation dominates, consistent with WP3.

## Results paragraph: the audit pays (visibility x damage)

Crossing the tolerance table with the blind diagnostics gives the payoff matrix of the audit. Every rung with material damage (|delta r| exceedance above 0.005: shifts of 0.5/1/2 px, rotations of 1/2 deg, and the blur on the real scan) is identified by the pre-registered rule at both angles; the rungs the rule cannot see (gains, angle belief, noise x0.5) are the rungs that do not hurt. Formally one rung lands in the dangerous invisible-and-harmful cell, gain x1.5, but its band exceedance is 0.0002 in r, at the resolution of the 12-seed band itself. Within that resolution the conclusion of the work package is a clean sentence: for this instrument, the defects the audit can catch are exactly the defects the practitioner needs to fear, and they are the geometric ones.

## Honest caveats

1. gain_proj is post-hoc and labelled so; the pre-registered rule scored 0/80 on the gain family.
2. The null is only as good as the calibration uncertainty it encodes; a mis-stated JITTER moves every threshold.
3. The identification is at the family level; magnitudes are not estimated (except gain_proj, which is a slope estimate).
4. Each non-borderline rung is one net with one shared seed (differences are the simulator by construction); the two borderline rungs (0.5 px shift, blur) were re-trained with three extra seeds each and their real-scan damage holds for every seed.
5. One scan, one instrument, one painting.
