# WP1 results text for the paper (draft, numbers from results/wp1_summary.txt and results/wp1_adaptive_summary.txt, run of 2026-08-28)

All numbers below are means over the 8 element lines and, for the simulated cases, over the 32 held-out cases (angle 8/14/20/25 deg x hole none/14x20 x test simulator validated/sharp x dose 1.0/0.5, all from prova2, never in training). The real anchor is the measured tilted scan (ruotato, 7.7 deg) restored and scored against prova2. 12 + 12 members, ~10 min each on CPU, 469k parameters each.

## Method paragraph (Section 3.x, Misspecification-aware uncertainty)

We quantify what an imperfect simulator leaves behind with two deep ensembles of the residual U-Net. The *jitter* ensemble draws, for every member, a different simulator from the calibration posterior of the instrument model: the noise law scale (log-sd 0.33, the transfer of the calibrated Var = k*counts to the tilted session), the tilt-gain slope (sd 20 %, the mounting angle being an upper bound), a per-line gain offset (sd 1.0 %, the session repeatability floor), the mounting angle (sd 0.5 deg) and the registration translation and rotation (sd 0.1 px, 0.3 deg, the spread of the per-element affine fits). Every sigma is traced to a measured quantity of the calibration chain (config.py). The *control* ensemble uses the nominal simulator with the same twelve seeds, so it captures initialisation and training-noise variance only; the excess spread of the jitter ensemble is, by construction, the variance attributable to simulator uncertainty. Members are trained on prova1 only and never see prova2 or the real scan. The predictive band adds the propagated measurement noise, obtained by re-simulating each test case with fresh noise (8 replicates) and reading the spread of the ensemble mean; on the real scan the calibrated noise of the reference map (k*truth) is added, because the reference is itself a measurement. No finite-ensemble inflation is applied (c4 = 0.978 at N = 12).

## Results paragraph (Section 4.x)

**The ensemble knows where the simulator is uncertain.** On the footprint the jitter ensemble spreads 2.2x (simulated) and 2.8x (real scan) wider than the control ensemble; 62 % (simulated) and 86 % (real) of its variance is attributable to the simulator draw rather than to initialisation. The simulator share grows monotonically with the tilt angle, from 104 counts rms at 8 deg to 148 at 25 deg (spread ratio 2.0 to 2.4), exactly the direction in which the measured per-line gains are extrapolated from the single 7.7 deg calibration. The spread maps (Fig. wp1_spread_maps) show the difference in character: the control spread is confined to the dropout hole, the jitter spread follows the painting's edges and level structure, where a registration or gain error shows.

**Calibration on the footprint.** With ensemble spread alone both ensembles under-cover (at z = 2: jitter 0.75 simulated / 0.53 real, control 0.44 / 0.26 against the Gaussian 0.95), as expected when the dominant error is measurement noise that no ensemble can see. Adding the simulator-propagated noise, the jitter band reaches 0.64 / 0.88 (z = 1 / 2) on the simulated cases, bracketed from above at 0.85 / 0.97 when the reference-map noise is also counted (the truth of the bracket lies in between, because part of the reference noise passes through the forward warp into the input). On the real scan, where the reference-noise term is unambiguous, the jitter band covers 0.61 / 0.87 / 0.95 at z = 1 / 2 / 3 (target 0.68 / 0.95 / 1.00) against 0.53 / 0.80 / 0.91 for the control ensemble: the jitter ensemble is close to calibrated, the control ensemble is not, and the difference is the simulator's share. The binned reliability diagram (Fig. wp1_error_vs_sigma) shows the jitter spread following the diagonal up to about 0.2 line-std, the control spread lying above it throughout. The jitter spread also ranks the errors better on the real scan (Spearman(sigma, |err|) 0.30 vs 0.25, AUSE 0.33 vs 0.38) and marginally on the simulated cases (0.36 vs 0.33).

**Where it fails: missing data.** Inside a 14 x 20 dropout hole neither band is calibrated (z = 2 coverage 0.49 jitter, 0.60 control; rms error 940 counts against a spread of 320 to 410), the spread ranks the error no better than chance (Spearman 0.08 to 0.13), and the jitter ensemble is *narrower* than the control ensemble in the hole (ratio 0.85). Simulator jitter perturbs the instrument model, not the image prior: all members share the same prova1 prior and agree on the same smooth fill, so the ensemble is confidently wrong. Uncertainty over a wrong simulator and uncertainty over a wrong prior are different objects, and a hole exposes the second one.

**Jitter training costs nothing on the real scan.** The mean of the jitter ensemble matches or exceeds the deterministic inverse in r on 5 of 8 lines on the real scan (the other three within 0.002) while restoring contrast (cv_ratio 0.979 vs 0.947), whereas the nominal-simulator MVP net and the control-ensemble mean lose r on 8 of 8 lines, the negative result of our v2 iteration. Jitter acts as a physics-consistent augmentation against exactly the residual mismatch the real scan has. The price is paid in the hole (r 0.26 vs 0.33 for the control mean) and in a 0.003 loss of r on the simulated footprint, where the nominal simulator is the true one by construction.

**Uncertainty-guided acquisition (stretch).** Using the jitter spread to choose the next 5 x 5 tiles of the tilted frame, an r of 0.90 (mean over Ca, Cu, PbLa, three simulated cases) is reached after measuring 79 % of the frame, against 85 % for random or raster order and 72 % for an oracle that sees the true error; for r = 0.95 the fractions are 88 %, 93 % and 82 %. The spread therefore buys about half of the oracle's saving, roughly 6 % of the pixels (dose and time) at the quality of a full scan, and it does so from the first step, where only the prior can guide the scanner.

## The three sentences (if only three fit)

A twelve-member ensemble trained across simulators drawn within the measured calibration uncertainty spreads 2.8x wider on the real scan than a nominal-simulator ensemble, 86 % of that spread is attributable to the simulator, and with the propagated measurement noise it covers 0.61 / 0.87 / 0.95 of the real-scan pixels at 1 / 2 / 3 sigma against 0.53 / 0.80 / 0.91 for the nominal ensemble. Its mean is also the first learned restoration that keeps the deterministic inverse's correlation on the real scan (5/8 lines at or above, the rest within 0.002; the nominal net loses 8/8) while restoring contrast. Inside a dropout hole neither ensemble is calibrated and the jittered one is narrower: simulator uncertainty is not prior uncertainty, and a missing-data region exposes the difference.

## Honest caveats to keep in the text

1. The calibration bracket on simulated cases (total vs total + reference noise) exists because the truth maps are measurements; the real-scan panel does not have this ambiguity and is the one to quote.
2. The jitter sigmas are 1-sigma proxies; the gain-slope uncertainty is one-sided in reality (the mounting angle is an upper bound) and we symmetrised it, so the jitter is a lower bound on the true simulator uncertainty.
3. 12 members: the sample std has a 2 % bias and the z = 2 band of a t(11) would be 10 % wider; neither changes a conclusion.
4. The oracle in the adaptive experiment ranks tiles by the error summed over the three headline lines, so per-line oracle curves are not optimal per line (visible on PbLa).
5. Everything is one painting, one instrument; the transfer of the jitter recipe to another instrument needs its own calibration chain.
